PCB Manufacturing and Assembly with Fusion and JLCPCB

This guide prepares a released Fusion Electronics design for fabrication and optional assembly. JLCPCB is a manufacturer to consider, not a course requirement. If you choose another service, use that manufacturer's current file formats, capabilities, and order rules.

The standard course recommendation is a four-layer, 1.6 mm board with top-side assembly. A two-layer board can be a reasonable cost-saving choice. Your exported layers and order settings must match the board you actually designed; do not select four layers merely because an example did.

Complete the design release review before generating the final package.

Note

The instructor checked the Gerber, placement, and BOM export paths below in their current Fusion version on 2026-10-08. Students use their own installations and are expected to keep Fusion up to date; menu labels may differ slightly between versions. Review the generated files and the manufacturer's upload preview for each design.

Decide the Assembly Scope Before Release

Classify every component while selecting parts, not after routing:

Classification Meaning Required record
Vendor assembled Included in the assembly order Exact supplier/manufacturer part number, package, designators, side, and placement data
Group assembled Installed after delivery Procurement source, package, orientation, and assembly plan
DNP Intentionally not populated Designators, electrical default state, and reason

If using JLCPCB assembly, verify current stock, package, specifications, price, and service eligibility. An LCSC number in a library does not prove that the chosen part is correct or available. Preserve the manufacturer part number as well as the distributor/service identifier where practical.

Prefer surface-mount devices (SMDs) when they meet the electrical and mechanical needs. Automated pick-and-place and reflow make ordinary SMD placement inexpensive. Plated through-hole (PTH) parts require manual insertion, hand soldering, or a separate wave-soldering process and can add substantial labor cost. Use PTH connectors or other parts when their mechanical advantages justify that cost, and consider whether the group should install them after delivery.

JLCPCB classifies frequently used components as Basic parts and many other components as Extended parts. Basic and selected promotional/preferred Extended parts can avoid feeder-loading fees, while other Extended parts can add a per-part-type setup fee. Individual components can also carry minimum purchase quantities and additional attrition quantities for assembly loss. These categories, fees, minimums, and attrition allowances change; compare electrically acceptable candidates in the live parts library and review the actual quote rather than choosing from an old list.

Top-side-only assembly is the course recommendation because it often reduces cost and complexity, but use the configuration that the design and selected service require.

Create a Release Archive

Keep the source revision and generated outputs together. A useful archive includes:

  • schematic and PCB source revision;
  • fabrication outputs and drill files;
  • bill of materials (BOM);
  • component placement list (CPL), also called pick-and-place or centroid data;
  • assembly drawings or notes;
  • DNP and group-assembled part lists;
  • screenshots or exported reports from the final upload review;
  • selected order settings and any approved substitutions.

Regenerate all outputs from the same frozen revision. Do not mix a newer BOM with older Gerbers or placement data.

Generate Fabrication Outputs

Use Board Editor → Manufacturing → CAM Processor → Select Job → Process Job → ZIP file. Select an output job appropriate to the chosen manufacturer and stackup, then generate the ZIP containing the Gerber and drill outputs.

Inspect the generated file list rather than assuming that a template fits the design. At minimum, the package normally represents:

  • each copper layer actually used;
  • top and bottom solder mask as applicable;
  • top and bottom silkscreen as applicable;
  • board profile or outline;
  • plated and non-plated drill information as required;
  • paste layers when the assembly or stencil process requires them.

A two-layer design should not export fictitious inner copper layers. A four-layer design must include its two real inner layers with the intended ordering.

Open the result in a Gerber viewer and inspect every layer, the outline, drills, mask openings, silkscreen, and alignment. The manufacturer's upload viewer is a second interpretation to review, not a replacement for checking local outputs.

Prepare the BOM and CPL

Use these export paths in the Board Editor:

  1. Placement data: Manufacturing → PickAndPlace → .mnt file → Edit Manually → CPL file. Preserve the raw export, then arrange its placement data into the supplier's CPL columns and save the final file as CSV. Changing the filename extension alone does not convert the file. Include the designator, X and Y coordinates, assembly side, and rotation; check each against the board.
  2. Bill of materials: Document → Bill of Materials → .csv file → Edit Manually → BOM file. Preserve the raw export, then arrange the columns for the supplier and add or verify exact part identifiers. Include only the intended vendor-assembled parts.

Check that the placement export includes every part intended for vendor assembly and that the final CPL identifies the correct side for each part. For a two-sided assembly order, include both sides in the same CPL. Preserve explicit and consistent units, and review the actual generated columns against JLCPCB's file-preparation guidance.

For JLCPCB assembly, the BOM identifies what to place and the CPL identifies where and how to place it. Reference designators must agree exactly between them. JLCPCB's standard format uses millimeter coordinates and a layer/side field. ME5305 designs are normally laid out in mils in Fusion, so handle the conversion explicitly rather than allowing bare mil values to be interpreted as millimeters.

Illustrative BOM structure:

Comment,Designator,Footprint,LCSC Part #
10 kΩ,"R1,R2,R3,R4",0603,Cxxxxxx
100 nF,"C1,C2",0603,Cyyyyyy

Illustrative CPL structure:

Designator,Mid X,Mid Y,Layer,Rotation
R1,38.100,38.608,Top,0
R2,40.640,38.608,Top,0
R3,43.180,38.608,Top,0
R4,45.720,38.608,Top,0
C1,25.400,43.180,Top,90
C2,27.940,43.180,Top,90

The example above uses millimeters. A course-tested alternative is to retain Fusion's mil coordinates and append the unit directly to every X and Y value:

Designator,Mid X,Mid Y,Layer,Rotation
R1,1500mil,1520mil,Top,0
R2,1600mil,1520mil,Top,0
R3,1700mil,1520mil,Top,0
R4,1800mil,1520mil,Top,0
C1,1000mil,1700mil,Top,90
C2,1100mil,1700mil,Top,90

Do not label bare mil values with millimeter headers. Whichever form you use, keep one coordinate unit throughout the file and verify the board scale, origin, and every component position in JLCPCB's upload preview.

The BOM and both CPL examples describe the same six components. The part numbers above are placeholders, not recommendations. Quote a CSV field that contains commas. Validate that:

  • every assembled designator appears once in the BOM and once in the CPL;
  • letter case and spelling match;
  • DNP and group-assembled parts are excluded or handled according to the service workflow;
  • package and value match the selected part;
  • units, origin, side, and rotation are consistent;
  • no two different parts share one designator;
  • the viewer places every component at the expected location and orientation.

Fusion and the assembly service may use different rotation conventions or library origins. The uploaded placement preview is therefore a required design interpretation check, especially for IC pin one and polarized parts.

Configure the Order

Upload the fabrication package and choose settings that match the released design. Review layer count, thickness, copper weight, finish, solder-mask color, quantity, panelization, and any controlled-impedance or special-process options actually used.

If ordering assembly:

  1. Select the intended assembly service and side or sides.
  2. Upload the BOM and CPL from the same release archive.
  3. Resolve unmatched or substituted parts by checking electrical specifications, package, orientation, lifecycle, and stock.
  4. Verify which parts are vendor assembled, group assembled, and DNP.
  5. Inspect every placement in the assembly viewer, not only the entries with warnings.
  6. Review board orientation, pin one, polarity, side, rotation, and omitted parts.
  7. Save evidence of the final configuration with the release archive.

JLCPCB's current standard-assembly guidance includes service-specific edge-rail and fiducial rules. These rules can change and may depend on panelization or service type. Check the current requirements for the actual order instead of retaining a universal “two or three fiducials” or 1 mm component-edge rule.

Final Package Review

  • Source revision and all output files identify the same design.
  • Fabrication layers match the selected stackup.
  • Outline, drills, copper, mask, paste, and silkscreen render correctly.
  • BOM and CPL designators reconcile exactly.
  • Assembly scope distinguishes vendor-assembled, group-assembled, and DNP parts.
  • Every selected part was checked for exact specifications and package.
  • Placement side, origin, rotation, pin one, and polarity were inspected in the upload viewer.
  • Order settings match the released board.
  • Cost, quantity, shipping, and lead time were reviewed before purchase.
  • The final package and review evidence were archived.

During manufacturing lead time, prepare the bring-up plan and firmware needed for initial tests.
